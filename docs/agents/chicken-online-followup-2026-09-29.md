# Chicken identifier gate: independent published equivalences

Date: 2026-09-29. Scope: bounded online follow-up for ticket 04; no mapping has been accepted or applied by this note.

## A concrete new mapping source

Degalez and colleagues' [2024 primary chicken annotation study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10951430/) explicitly publishes a cross-assembly identifier correspondence in [Supplementary Table 12, publisher-hosted XLSX](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41598-024-56705-y/MediaObjects/41598_2024_56705_MOESM15_ESM.xlsx) (13,515,843 bytes; the [PMC copy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/instance/10951430/bin/41598_2024_56705_MOESM15_ESM.xlsx) is also listed). The article says the table relates RefSeq and Ensembl identifiers on GRCg7b, Ensembl identifiers on GRCg7b and GRCg7w, and their earlier galgal5/GRCg6a annotation identifiers to GRCg7b. The GRCg6a atlas was anchored to **Ensembl release 101**. The article gives an explicit example: old `ENSGALG00000004965` and new `ENSGALG00010027394` denote SLC27A4, with a third GRCg7w ID. This is directly relevant because the checkpoint's 16,878 chicken keys exactly match the selected release-101 GRCg6a gene set in the [existing provenance audit](chicken-identifier-provenance-2026-09-28.md#article-code-and-peptide-follow-up--2026-09-29).

The publisher's [supplementary legends](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41598-024-56705-y/MediaObjects/41598_2024_56705_MOESM21_ESM.docx) describe two forms in Table 12: gene-by-gene equivalence with coordinates in both assemblies, and a gene-ID-only list collapsed with GRCg7b as reference. This is an **independent research-group correspondence**, not the TranscriptFormer producer's checkpoint provenance or an Ensembl stable-ID history export. Its relationships may be derived from that group's enriched atlas and need row-level inspection. A citation to the article alone does not establish a unique, correct mapping for any of the 9,611 unresolved checkpoint genes.

The study's GRCg7b Ensembl annotation is v107, while the local ortholog table
uses Ensembl Compara v110; identical new-prefix spelling alone does not certify
that every gene model stayed equivalent across that release interval.

A bounded audit of the publisher XLSX (SHA-256 `45498dac89c430439817df71b469a205f2fe68e180b6dc3f881f69c3527ced4c`) streamed the 56,871 data rows in `GRCg6a_GRCg7b_geneByGene`. Only **2,654** rows have exact, unversioned old-prefix and new-prefix Ensembl gene IDs in columns A and B; two additional old IDs carry `.V94` suffixes and were excluded from this exact-key audit. Requiring uniqueness in both directions among those Ensembl-to-Ensembl rows and an exact checkpoint key leaves **224** source rows. Of those, 40 overlap the existing GeneID/RefSeq bridge: **3 agree and 37 disagree**. Another 28 point to a gene in the local ortholog table, while 156 do not. These are limited to the direct Ensembl-ID subset; other identifiers and gene-model collapses in the publisher's atlas can introduce further ambiguity. No candidate was added to the bridge.

The reproducible [audit command](../../scripts/audit_chicken_published_mapping.py), [224 row candidates with coordinates](../chicken-published-table12-candidates.tsv), and [hash-bound summary](../chicken-published-table12-audit.json) retain source row numbers, old/new coordinates and conflicting bridge IDs. The audit reads the exact checkpoint HDF5 without loading its embedding arrays or a cell expression matrix. The conflict rate makes the publication a useful **review lead**, not an accepted repair or a substitute for the checkpoint's source provenance.

## Further evidence needed

1. Reconcile the 37 conflicting pairs against original gene models, exon structure and independent cross-assembly evidence. The article's [methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10951430/) use genomic remapping and one-base, same-strand exon overlap while aggregating annotations; a row is not proof of a unique Ensembl stable-ID lineage.
2. Examine the collapsed form of Table 12 and any non-Ensembl aliases before claiming biological one-to-one status. Compare proposed additions with both actual vocabularies, not just the ortholog table.
3. Keep any eventually corroborated additions as a separately named mapping layer with source row, evidence, ambiguity exclusions and scientific review. The 28 direct candidates with an ortholog-table join do not themselves pass this gate.

Ensembl's [stable-ID documentation](https://jun2026.archive.ensembl.org/info/genome/stable_ids/index.html) says its identifier propagation uses location and exon-based comparisons during reannotation. The [release-107 chicken annotation report](https://www.ensembl.org/info/genome/genebuild/2022_05_Gallus_gallus_gene_annotation.pdf) confirms GRCg7b and GRCg6a as distinct assemblies and says identifiers are propagated by comparing new and prior gene sets. Its description does not supply a ready-made old/new gene table. [Ensembl's core schema](https://rc-metazoa.ensembl.org/info/docs/api/core/core_schema.html) defines `mapping_session` and `stable_id_event` with old/new ID, release and assembly fields; the existing ticket audit found no usable cross-assembly events in the inspected release-107 core tables. These sources support seeking an actual mapping export or independently checking candidates, rather than rewriting ID prefixes.

An [Ensembl REST assembly-map endpoint](https://rest.ensembl.org/documentation/info/assembly_map) converts coordinates between assemblies. A coordinate lift can offer locus evidence for candidates, but it is not a gene-identity mapping and must be checked for split, duplicate and multi-locus cases. [NCBI's Gene documentation](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/datasets/docs/v2/data-processing/gene-processing/gene/) explains why GeneIDs can persist across genome updates while also allowing record suppression and secondary IDs. The current strict GeneID/RefSeq bridge therefore remains a defensible partial baseline, not an exhaustive bridge.

The [TranscriptFormer preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.04.25.650731v1) names Ensembl v113 for mapped gene features, whereas the [later public peptide manifest](https://github.com/czi-ai/transcriptformer/blob/181193c/preprocess/fasta_manifest_pep.json) lists chicken release-113 GRCg7b. As detailed in the [existing audit](chicken-identifier-provenance-2026-09-28.md#article-code-and-peptide-follow-up--2026-09-29), neither source binds an annotation FASTA to the released chicken HDF5. The exact producer release remains unresolved even if Table 12 yields more valid cross-assembly pairs.
